## Source operations and reusable requirements

### Splicing and pore transport

The source maintains a diagonal green RNA backbone with rounded, separately colored bases. Two blue enzyme forms approach separated positions on it. A long intron emphasis hold precedes bending into a loop; retained exons stay legible while the loop and machinery depart. This requires explicit topology changes, persistent nucleotide identities, separated cut ends, a visible replacement connection, and an independently moving removed segment. Fading a middle interval is insufficient.

The pore passage changes representation to a simpler green segmented strand and establishes an orange lobed opening through a purple membrane. The camera changes which side of the opening is visible while the same flexible strand passes through. A reusable transport helper should map stable sites to arc distance on an authored path and support membrane-side queries; the proof must demonstrate actual near/far occlusion and a hole in the membrane geometry.

## Independent acceptance still required

These captures supply choreography evidence; they do not approve a library implementation. Each capability needs bounded validation, exact identity/topology checks across arbitrary seeks, native films at 16:9 and 4:3, full-duration motion inspection, direct source comparison, and measured scene/runtime costs. Source-specific materials and composition remain distinct from mathematical and algorithmic styles. Depth-aware optical focus remains a separate capability gap until implemented and demonstrated; dimming background objects alone does not establish optical blur.
