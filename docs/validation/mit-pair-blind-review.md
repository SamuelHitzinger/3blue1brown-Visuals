The source supports the displayed relation ẍ(t)=−(k/m)x(t)=−ω²x(t). Both versions explicitly assume positive k/m and identify their sweep as a position comparison rather than a physical trajectory. That is appropriate restraint: neither invents a spring apparatus, numerical parameters, or a time solution. The positive and negative position samples show acceleration pointing toward zero, opposite the cyan displacement vector. Both arrows shrink into the crossing and reverse across it; the 3.49/3.5/3.51 samples show no surviving directional arrow at zero. This earns the narrow sign-comparison claim, though it does not explain Newton's law or derive harmonic motion. Those broader claims are not required by this brief.

For A: reasoning is **ready** for that narrow claim; visual readability is **ready** in the supplied samples; reference style is **revise**; technical delivery is **unverified** beyond the inspected PNGs. The thin leader from the white marker to the yellow acceleration arrow clarifies attachment at 0 and 8 seconds, and the zero tick, marker, and cyan displacement anchor remain identifiable through the crossing. Labels are legible at both sizes. However, the centered complete equation at the top, centered number line in the middle, and permanent explanatory footer form essentially the rejected presentation structure. The white equation does not connect its ẍ and x terms to the yellow and cyan objects, and its negative multiplier never becomes a visible operation. The formula is already complete at 0 seconds and remains a detached reference at 8 seconds. A short local construction showing a displacement copy scaled and reversed by −k/m, with stable colored term correspondence, would address this mismatch. Recheck the complete 0–8 second passage after revision; merely adding a grid or changing yellow to a reference hue would not.

For B: reasoning is **ready** for the same narrow claim; visual readability is **ready**, with a weaker attachment cue; reference style is **revise**; technical delivery is **unverified** beyond the inspected PNGs. It shares A's main layout and algebra-to-object shortcomings. Its acceleration arrow floats above the marker with no leader, so the association depends on horizontal alignment. This is particularly weak at 3.25 and 3.75 seconds, when the arrow is short. Add a quiet connection or place the arrow directly at its referent, while retaining adequate separation from the displacement arrow. The dim +x label is less legible than A's axis label, although the positive direction is still inferable. These are smaller differences than the shared style failure.

The one-dimensional representation and sparse scaffold are appropriate to this source; no imitation Euler grid, rotating complex vector, or extra subject coverage is needed. What is missing is the reference's transferable mechanism: meaningful object correspondence, a visible operation on that object, and algebra developing at the object. Stable cyan/yellow roles and serif math are useful ingredients, but do not establish that mechanism. A compact zero-value label or brief zero hold would also help distinguish the intended vanishing vectors from a missing drawing at the crossing.

The sample positions suggest an initial hold from 0 to 1 second and a final hold from 6 to 8 seconds. Samples alone cannot establish easing, uninterrupted object continuity, temporal polish, or actual playback holds, and no final movie was inspected. There is no justified winner by motion quality.
