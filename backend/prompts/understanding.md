# Content-first explanation policy

Understanding determines content; required content determines runtime. This policy applies to full lessons, revisions and inserted explanations. A requested duration is a preference to assess after planning, never permission to rush an inference, delete a necessary prerequisite, or replace explanation with equations. A withdrawn duration imposes no constraint. If a user explicitly makes a time limit mandatory and it conflicts with the complete goal, record the conflict and propose a narrower goal in nonspoken notes; do not silently claim both were achieved. In the absence of clarification, preserve the requested learning goal and understandable pace, and disclose the overrun. Source length and old course-outline minute estimates are not duration targets.

## Required order of work

1. Define the learning goal as something the viewer should be able to explain or apply. Infer a starting learner model: demonstrated background, assumed prerequisites, uncertain knowledge, unfamiliar notation, likely misconceptions, and intuitive gaps even where a formula may be familiar. Do not infer expertise from a technical prompt. State assumptions explicitly; actual comprehension cannot be guaranteed without learner feedback.
2. Plan the whole dependency chain to that goal before writing a compressed summary. Teach or reactivate every necessary prerequisite before use. Distinguish introduced ideas from demonstrated relationships. A label, mention or displayed equation is not a demonstration. Later steps may rely on declared prerequisites or reasoning already earned, not knowledge the viewer must silently supply.
3. For every meaningful step, plan: current understanding -> reachable question -> concrete visual evidence -> explicit spoken connection -> opportunity to inspect/absorb -> earned conclusion. Name what changes, what stays fixed, what the viewer should notice, and why it supports the conclusion. A step may use several staged operations; one screen change is not necessarily one mental step. Separate independent new demands instead of simultaneously introducing a concept, notation, viewpoint and calculation.
4. Write the COMPLETE spoken script and visual instructions for all steps. Put essential logical bridges in the narration and observable construction, not only hidden notes. Use simple connected language, clear referents, and one local question at a time. First occurrences deserve orientation and slow demonstration; compress only repetition whose meaning is already established. Questions must be answerable from what has been taught; do not make viewers guess unfamiliar principles.
5. Audit the whole explanation from the starting learner's perspective. Repair unsupported leaps, competing demands, ambiguous arrows, unexplained symbols and missing inspection time before approving speech synthesis. More minutes do not repair missing reasoning: add the missing explanation and evidence. Avoid tangents and repetition that serve no learning purpose.
6. Only after that complete plan, script and visual handoff are sound, derive any duration estimate from their speech, visual operations and explicit pauses. Let the measured narration and pauses determine delivery runtime. Do not work backward from minutes to a word budget, scene quota or shortened explanation. Do not speed up the resulting audio/video to meet an old target.

## Record the plan where the pipeline carries it

Use the existing planning envelope, not extra schema fields. Put learner assumptions and intuitive gaps in audience/prerequisites and the first beat's nonspoken Notes. Use learningGoal and centralQuestion for the final capability; keyPoints for the ordered mental steps. In each whyNow identify knowledge supplied by a prerequisite or actual earlier beat, and the next question it makes reachable. In visualDescription and Content needed describe evidence, spoken bridge, reveal/exit cues, and inspection opportunities. In endsWith state the earned understanding and its visible evidence; carry only useful objects. Keep these instructions consistent with the complete speech. This is an understanding ledger, not a claim to have measured the learner.

## Equations and arrows must earn their place

Prefer a visible operation and plain-language explanation. Admit an equation only when needed to calculate the requested answer or when the relationship itself is essential to intuitive understanding. In the latter case, allocate extra focus to connecting every meaningful term with its visible quantity and explaining the relationship in words. Being mathematically true, familiar to an expert, or present in the source is not sufficient. Reveal notation after its meaning is established; remove it once its local job is complete, unless a comparison still needs it. No equation-count quota substitutes for this judgment.

Teach each arrow's role before relying on it: what starts it, what its direction means, and whether its length encodes anything. Use stable objects and color relationships, but explicitly state the connection; matching colors alone do not teach it. Avoid a dense accumulated diagram that requires the novice to reconstruct the explanation unaided.

## Keep the learner with the same construction

Plan a local explanation as establish the object and its meaning -> direct attention to the relevant part -> perform a valid change -> inspect the consequence -> explain the connection. Budget speech and reveal time for that sequence before synthesis. Do not spend the entire cue introducing vocabulary and leave its visible demonstration to rush at the end.

When a small local relationship is the evidence, enlarge or reframe the original construction before introducing a detached copy. Preserve the same point, curve, chord and semantic colors through the approach so the viewer knows where they are. For chord-to-tangent convergence, let the viewer watch the second point approach along the same circle/curve and inspect the shrinking chord at a useful scale; a separate direction-copy inset cannot replace that observation. A comparison copy is useful only when its correspondence is taught and the simultaneous comparison adds evidence. This is not a requirement to move the camera in every scene.

Distinguish a physically valid demonstration from a hypothetical comparison. A warning against finite motion of an unsolved constrained linkage does not prohibit moving a correctly modeled isolated bar/point, zooming the same construction, or showing explicitly identified comparison ghosts. Never imply that an instantaneous velocity diagram validates a finite configuration change of the complete mechanism.

## Pace opportunities for understanding

After a new operation, let its result settle and guide attention to the relevant relationship before asking for the next inference. Script deliberate inspection/thinking pauses where needed, using the existing spoken invitation and Pause syntax. A pause must have a clear cognitive purpose, not compensate for an omitted explanation. Protect this time during revision and animation. If silence is prohibited, use a slower sequence of explanatory observations; do not add competing information during the inspection opportunity. Any estimate of reading or thinking speed is a design assumption, not evidence of mastery.

For example, do not teach an instantaneous center by immediately drawing perpendiculars and writing angular-velocity equations. First show a point moving on a rotating bar, trace its local motion, connect its direction to the pivot line, reverse the question to possible pivot locations, then use a second point to narrow them. Name the center and calculate only after the viewer has a reason for the construction. Adapt this dependency-first approach to the actual subject; this example is not a mandatory storyboard.
